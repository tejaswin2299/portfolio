Artifact 6 · Workshop Six

Leading Responsible AI Change Through Trust and Transparency

A personal framework that connects technical judgment, stakeholder participation, ethical governance, measurable action, and continuous adaptation.

Purpose and transformation

I began the course seeing AI/ML leadership mainly as selecting an accurate model and organizing a technical team. I now understand that a technically strong system can still fail when employees do not trust its purpose, understand its limits, or have a meaningful role in its use.

Mission: My mission is to lead AI/ML change in a way that earns trust, protects people, and turns reliable data into decisions that create lasting organizational and community value.

Objective

Create a living guide for responsible AI leadership that joins people, ethics, organizational goals, and technical evidence.

Course application

A customer-service assistant scenario changed my thinking from speed and accuracy alone to a limited pilot, approved sources, citations, corrections, and clear human escalation.

Target audience

Technical teams, business leaders, compliance partners, end users, instructors, and hiring managers evaluating responsible-AI leadership.

Four core values

Transparency

I will explain purpose, data sources, known limits, and appropriate uses in language affected users can understand.

Human dignity and fairness

I will not reduce people to predictions. I will test outcomes across relevant groups and preserve human review for consequential decisions.

Accountability

I will make ownership visible: who approves, monitors, responds to incidents, and can stop a system.

Learning and humility

I will remain open to correction because models, data, organizational conditions, and community needs change.

Measurable objectives and action

Six months

Complete an advanced responsible-AI, model-evaluation, or governance course and apply three practices at work.

Every pilot

Create a stakeholder map and hold at least two feedback sessions before full deployment, recording concerns and responses.

One year

Implement a scorecard covering task accuracy, error severity, adoption, response time, fairness, and human escalation.

Every quarter

Present one short AI learning session and target at least 80% participant clarity about system purpose and limitations.

Short-term action

Study NIST and UNESCO guidance, interview users, define success and stop conditions, run small pilots, and provide weekly office hours.

Long-term action

Build cross-functional governance and reusable model cards, risk registers, test templates, and incident procedures.

Evaluation and adaptation

I will review the framework every three months, score communication, ethical judgment, collaboration, technical learning, and adaptability from one to five, and request anonymous feedback from at least three technical and nontechnical colleagues.

Decision rule: Low adoption triggers investigation of usefulness, workload, training, and trust. Missed fairness or safety thresholds pause expansion and require revision.
