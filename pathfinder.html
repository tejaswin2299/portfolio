Human-centered AI design

ML PathFinder AI

A beginner-friendly planning coach that uses a structured PATH conversation to connect the problem, data, testing, and ethical review.

← All artifacts
PATH

Problem and purpose

Beginning machine-learning students may have an idea or dataset but still struggle to define a target, choose a valid metric, prevent leakage, or set a realistic scope. ML PathFinder AI was designed as a planning coach that guides the reasoning without producing a complete graded submission.

Primary user

Students and early-career learners planning a first machine-learning project.

Value

Turns an unclear idea into a smaller, explainable, testable, and responsible project blueprint.

Boundary

Supports planning, explanation, and review while leaving verification and final work with the learner.

The conversation framework

P

Problem & people

Clarify the real question, user, affected people, and intended decision or learning result.

A

Assess the data

Review target, features, permissions, missingness, time order, sensitive fields, and leakage risk.

T

Technique & testing

Select a baseline, no more than two candidate methods, a valid split, and meaningful metrics.

H

Human & ethical review

Check privacy, fairness, explainability, limitations, academic integrity, and human oversight.

Design and testing evidence

Repeated prompts

Three independent bike-demand project plans were compared for alignment, technical soundness, feasibility, ethics, and clarity.

Adaptive dialogue

The assistant was tested for preserving constraints while revising models, metrics, leakage checks, and explanations.

Failure conditions

Testing covered vague ideas, missing targets, imbalanced classes, time leakage, sensitive student data, and requests for complete assignments.

Artifact reflection

I selected ML PathFinder AI because it shows how I move from an ambiguous problem to a structured and responsible solution. The artifact combines user needs, prompt testing, research, design thinking, technical evaluation, and clear boundaries. It also reflects my data-engineering mindset because a useful model plan depends on trustworthy inputs, traceable decisions, and repeatable processes.

The most important lesson was that the best machine-learning plan does not start with the most advanced algorithm. It starts with a clear problem, appropriate data, valid testing, and honest limits. In future work, I would add usability testing with beginners and measure whether they can explain the final plan in their own words, not only whether the assistant completed a checklist.
