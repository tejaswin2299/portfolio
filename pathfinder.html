ML PathFinder AI

A beginner-friendly machine-learning planning coach that helps a learner move from a vague idea or unfamiliar dataset to a feasible, explainable, and responsible project blueprint.

Project purpose

Beginning machine-learning students can often find algorithms and code examples, but they may not know how to connect a real question, a dataset, a valid evaluation plan, and responsible use into one manageable project. ML PathFinder AI was designed to guide that reasoning without completing the student's graded work.

Audience

Students, career changers, and early-career professionals learning how to plan a first machine-learning project.

Problem solved

Unclear targets, unsuitable metrics, data leakage, unrealistic scope, and missing privacy or fairness review.

Success measure

The learner leaves with a smaller, clearer project, understands the reasoning, and knows the next three actions.

The PATH conversation framework

The four-stage framework keeps the assistant from jumping directly to an algorithm. Each stage makes an important part of the project visible.

P

Problem & people

Clarify the real question, intended user, affected people, prediction moment, and whether machine learning is appropriate.

A

Assess the data

Identify the target, unit of analysis, available features, time order, missingness, permissions, and leakage risks.

T

Technique & testing

Choose a simple baseline and no more than two candidate methods, then match the split and metrics to the task.

H

Human & ethical review

Review privacy, fairness, explainability, academic integrity, limitations, possible misuse, and human oversight.

Evidence and iteration

The design was informed by three repeated-prompt comparisons, an adaptive conversation with a specialized data-analysis GPT, a STORM research exercise on data leakage, and iterative prototype testing.

Prompt comparison

Three independent bike-demand project plans were compared for technical soundness, beginner feasibility, ethical awareness, and instructional clarity.

Research synthesis

The STORM workflow expanded leakage into target leakage, train-test contamination, time leakage, duplicate entities, and preprocessing errors.

Prototype evolution

The concept changed from a one-shot algorithm recommender to a guided coach with short intake questions, assumption labels, and high-impact-use warnings.

Scenario-based testing

The prototype was tested against normal and failure conditions. Each test required technical relevance, explanation, preservation of constraints, privacy awareness, and academic-integrity boundaries.

Vague project idea

Asked who would use the result, what outcome mattered, what data existed, and the deadline before recommending an algorithm.

No target variable

Explained that supervised learning requires a target and offered clustering or a revised data-collection question.

Imbalanced classification

Flagged misleading accuracy and recommended precision, recall, F1, PR-AUC, and a confusion matrix.

Time leakage

Recommended chronological holdout or time-series cross-validation instead of a random split.

Sensitive student data

Discouraged identifiable records, recognized the high-impact education context, and redirected toward aggregate support analysis.

Full graded assignment request

Declined a ready-to-submit deliverable while offering a blueprint, checklist, pseudocode, debugging, and feedback.

Responsible-use boundaryML PathFinder AI is a planning and learning assistant. It does not train or deploy models, make real decisions about people, invent data or citations, guarantee accuracy, or replace instructor requirements.

Design guardrails

  • Do not request unnecessary names, student IDs, medical details, addresses, credentials, or confidential employer data.
  • Do not recommend protected or sensitive attributes simply because they may improve model performance.
  • Treat healthcare, employment, lending, admissions, legal, and public-safety uses as high-impact contexts requiring stronger caution.
  • Clearly separate user-confirmed dataset fields from examples or assumptions.
  • Keep the final responsibility for verification, interpretation, and decisions with the human user.
Artifact reflection

What this work demonstrates

The strongest machine-learning plan does not begin with the most advanced algorithm. It begins with a clear human problem, trustworthy data, valid testing, and an honest understanding of risk.

Why I selected this artifact

I selected ML PathFinder AI because it demonstrates more than familiarity with generative AI. It shows that I can define a user problem, compare alternative responses, synthesize research, build a structured solution, establish boundaries, and test failure conditions. Those skills connect directly to data engineering because reliable machine learning depends on data quality, traceability, and disciplined processes.

Audience value

For instructors, the artifact documents learning and responsible AI practice. For employers and technical peers, it shows how I translate ambiguous needs into a practical workflow, communicate technical tradeoffs in plain language, and consider privacy, fairness, and operational risk before recommending a solution.

What I learned

The project changed my focus from choosing an algorithm to designing a complete decision process. The repeated-prompt exercise taught me to evaluate output using explicit criteria. The specialized GPT exercise showed the importance of preserving constraints during revision. The research activity showed that a well-organized response still requires source verification. Testing showed that ethical boundaries must be written into the design and examined directly.

Future improvement

A future version would include user testing with actual beginners, a dataset-readiness worksheet, a short progress-check mode, and optional examples for regression, classification, forecasting, and clustering. I would also measure whether users can explain the final plan in their own words rather than only whether they complete the conversation.
